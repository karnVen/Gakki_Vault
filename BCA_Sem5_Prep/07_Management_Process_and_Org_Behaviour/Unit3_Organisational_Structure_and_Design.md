## 1. Concept and Nature of Organisation Structure

### 1.1 Definition
> **Organisational Structure** is the formal system of task and reporting relationships that coordinates and motivates organizational members so they work together to achieve organizational goals.
> It defines how jobs are divided, grouped, coordinated, and how authority flows from top to bottom.

### 1.2 Purpose of an Organisation Structure
* Clarifies **who reports to whom** (prevents confusion and role ambiguity).
* Establishes channels of official communication.
* Prevents duplication of effort and overlapping duties.
* Facilitates seamless coordination between diverse specialist departments.

## 2. Six Fundamental Dimensions / Elements of Organisational Design

```
                     6 CORE ELEMENTS OF ORGANISATIONAL STRUCTURE
       ┌───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
       │ 1. Work Specialization    │ 2. Departmentalization    │ 3. Chain of Command       │
       │    (Division of Labor)    │    (Grouping of jobs)     │    (Line of Authority)    │
       ├───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
       │ 4. Span of Control        │ 5. Centralization vs.     │ 6. Formalization          │
       │    (Subordinates per boss)│    Decentralization       │    (Rules & Procedures)   │
       └───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Work Specialization (Division of Labour):** The degree to which organizational tasks are subdivided into separate, specialized jobs. Prevents workers from multitasking inefficiently.
2. **Departmentalization:** The basis by which individual jobs are grouped together:
   * *By Function:* Grouped by core expertise (Marketing, Finance, Engineering, HR).
   * *By Product:* Grouped around distinct product lines (e.g., Laptops, Smartphones, Tablets).
   * *By Geography / Territory:* Grouped by region (North India, European Division, APAC).
   * *By Customer Type:* Grouped by client demographic (Retail Consumer, Corporate B2B, Government).
3. **Chain of Command:** The unbroken line of authority extending from the top executive down to the lowest rank, establishing who is accountable to whom. Enforces:
   * **Authority:** Legitimate institutional right to issue instructions.
   * **Unity of Command:** Every employee receives orders from **only one** immediate superior.
4. **Span of Control:** The number of immediate subordinates a manager can direct efficiently and effectively.
5. **Centralization vs. Decentralization:**
   * **Centralization:** Decision-making power is concentrated tightly at the Top Management level.
   * **Decentralization:** Decision-making authority is systematically dispersed throughout middle and operational management.
6. **Formalization:** The degree to which jobs, processes, employee conduct, and workflows are standardized via written manuals, policies, and SOPs.

## 3. Span of Control & Tall vs. Flat Organisations (Guaranteed 5/10-Mark Question)

### 3.1 Narrow vs. Wide Span of Control

```
        TALL ORGANISATION (Narrow Span)                   FLAT ORGANISATION (Wide Span)
                  ┌───────┐                                         ┌───────┐
                  │  CEO  │                                         │  CEO  │
                  └───┬───┘                                         └───┬───┘
            ┌─────────┴─────────┐                         ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
            ▼                   ▼                         ▼             ▼             ▼
        ┌───────┐           ┌───────┐                 ┌───────┐     ┌───────┐     ┌───────┐
        │  VP 1 │           │  VP 2 │                 │ Mgr A │     │ Mgr B │     │ Mgr C │
        └───┬───┘           └───┬───┘                 └───────┘     └───────┘     └───────┘
         ┌──┴──┐             ┌──┴──┐                    (Supervises 10-15 workers each)
         ▼     ▼             ▼     ▼
       (Few subordinates per manager)
```

| Dimension | Tall Structure (Narrow Span) | Flat Structure (Wide Span) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Span of Control** | Small / Narrow (e.g., 3–5 subordinates per manager) | Large / Wide (e.g., 10–20+ subordinates per manager) |
| **Hierarchical Levels** | Many vertical tiers; steep pyramid | Few hierarchical tiers; compressed layout |
| **Communication** | Slow, filtered upward; prone to distortion through layers | Fast, direct communication between top and bottom |
| **Administrative Cost** | High (requires many salaried managers and supervisors) | Lower (fewer managerial overhead salaries) |
| **Employee Autonomy** | Close, tight supervision; low individual autonomy | High empowerment and autonomy; self-management |
| **Suitability** | Highly hazardous, complex, or strictly regulated tasks | Dynamic tech companies, startups, skilled professionals |

### 3.2 V.A. Graicunas Theory of Span of Control
French management consultant V.A. Graicunas mathematically proved that as the number of subordinates ($n$) increases arithmetically, the number of potential interpersonal relationships ($R$) increases geometrically:
$$R = n \left( 2^{n-1} + n - 1 \right)$$
* For $n = 4$ subordinates $\implies R = 44$ relationships.
* For $n = 6$ subordinates $\implies R = 222$ relationships!
* *Exam Note:* This formula demonstrates why a manager's span of control must remain bounded (typically 4–8 for administrative work).

## 4. Types / Forms of Organisational Structures

### 4.1 Line Organisation (Military / Scalar Structure)
* **Concept:** The oldest and simplest organizational form. Authority flows vertically downward in a direct, unbroken line from chief executive to operating employees.
* **Characteristics:** Direct scalar chain; complete unity of command; absolute authority at each level.
* **Advantages:** Simplicity, clear-cut lines of authority, instant decision-making, strict discipline.
* **Disadvantages:** Overburdens top executives; lacks specialized departmental knowledge; rigid and autocratic.
* **Suitability:** Small businesses, military units, and automated manufacturing with minimal product variations.

### 4.2 Line and Staff Organisation
* **Concept:** Combines the direct executive authority of **Line managers** with the advisory, specialized technical expertise of **Staff specialists**.
* **Distinction:**
  * **Line Managers:** Possess command authority; directly responsible for primary revenue-generating activities (Production, Sales).
  * **Staff Specialists:** Possess advisory authority; provide expert advice, legal counsel, R&D data, and HR support (Legal, Quality Audit, R&D).
* **Conflict ("Line vs Staff Conflict"):** Line managers complain staff advisors lack practical reality; staff complain line managers ignore expert recommendations.

### 4.3 Functional Organisation (F.W. Taylor's Specialized Division)
* **Concept:** Jobs are grouped strictly according to occupational specialization and enterprise business functions.
* **Structure:**
  ```
                          MANAGING DIRECTOR
                                  │
         ┌──────────────┬─────────┴────────┬──────────────┐
         ▼              ▼                  ▼              ▼
     FINANCE        MARKETING          OPERATIONS         HR
    DEPARTMENT      DEPARTMENT         DEPARTMENT     DEPARTMENT
  ```
* **Advantages:** High functional specialization; economies of scale within departments; clear professional career paths.
* **Disadvantages:** "Departmental Silos" (tunnel vision); poor inter-functional coordination; slow enterprise-wide decision making.

### 4.4 Divisional Structure (By Product, Market, or Geography)
* **Concept:** Organization is divided into semi-autonomous, self-contained business units (Strategic Business Units - SBUs), each containing its own functional teams (marketing, finance, ops).
* **Types:** Product-wise (Consumer Electronics vs Healthcare), Geographic (Asia-Pacific vs North America), Customer-wise (Retail vs Enterprise).
* **Advantages:** High accountability (each division is a standalone profit center); responsive to local market needs.
* **Disadvantages:** Duplication of expensive staff functions (every division hires separate HR and finance teams); intra-company rivalry.

### 4.5 Matrix Organisation (Hybrid Dual-Command Structure)
* **Concept:** Superimposes a horizontal project/product structure onto a vertical functional structure.
* **Core Distinction:** **Violates Henri Fayol's Unity of Command Principle!** An employee reports simultaneously to **TWO bosses**:
  1. Vertical Functional Manager (e.g., Head of Software Engineering).
  2. Horizontal Project Manager (e.g., Healthcare App Project Lead).

```
                        MATRIX ORGANISATION
                       Functional Hierarchy ───►
                      Engineering      Design         QA
   Project A ──►     [Eng + Proj A]  [Des + Proj A] [QA + Proj A]
   Project B ──►     [Eng + Proj B]  [Des + Proj B] [QA + Proj B]
```

* **Advantages:** Maximum flexibility; optimal sharing of highly specialized human experts across projects; fast adaptation to dynamic market needs.
* **Disadvantages:** High role ambiguity; constant power struggles and conflict between functional and project managers; stress on employees from dual reporting.

## 5. Formal vs. Informal Organisation (High-Yield 5/10-Mark Question)

| Basis of Comparison | Formal Organisation | Informal Organisation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin & Creation** | Deliberately designed by top management through official charts and policies | Emerges spontaneously through social interactions, shared interests, and human friendships |
| **Purpose** | To achieve formal organizational profitability and contractual targets | To satisfy human psychological, social, and belonging needs |
| **Authority** | Derived strictly from official position and hierarchy (flows downward) | Derived from personal charisma, technical respect, or seniority (flows horizontally/freely) |
| **Rules & Standards** | Explicit, documented written rules, SOPs, and codes of conduct | Unwritten group social norms, customs, and peer pressure |
| **Communication** | Structured, official chain of command (Scalar Chain) | Unstructured, rapid unofficial network (**Grapevine**) |
| **Flexibility** | Rigid, bureaucratic, and slow to change | Highly fluid, adaptive, and dynamic |
| **Stability** | Long-lasting and institutionalized | Volatile; forms, shifts, and dissolves easily |

### The Grapevine (Informal Communication Network)
Informal communication travels through 4 common patterns:
1. **Single Strand:** A tells B, who tells C (linear, prone to progressive distortion).
2. **Gossip Chain:** One person actively spreads information to everyone in the social circle.
3. **Probability Chain:** One person randomly transmits gossip to interested peers.
4. **Cluster Chain (Most Common):** Selected individuals pass information only to trusted confidants, who in turn pass it to their own select trusted circles.

## 6. Authority, Responsibility, Accountability & Delegation

### 6.1 The Triad of Governance
* **Authority:** The institutional, legitimate right to make decisions, direct actions, and allocate organizational resources.
* **Responsibility:** The obligatory duty of an employee to execute the assigned task to the best of their ability.
* **Accountability:** The ultimate answerability for the final outcome. **Authority can be delegated, responsibility can be shared, but absolute accountability CAN NEVER be delegated!**

### 6.2 The 3-Step Delegation Process
```
   [1. Assignment of Duties] ──> [2. Granting of Authority] ──> [3. Creation of Accountability]
```

### 6.3 Principles of Effective Delegation
1. **Principle of Parity of Authority and Responsibility:** Authority granted must be directly proportional to the responsibility assigned (excess authority breeds tyranny; insufficient authority breeds paralysis).
2. **Unity of Command:** Subordinates should be accountable to a single delegator.
3. **Scalar Principle:** Unbroken line of communication from top to bottom.
4. **Exception Principle (Management by Exception):** Routine matters should be resolved by lower delegates; only exceptional, unprecedented problems should be escalated upward.
