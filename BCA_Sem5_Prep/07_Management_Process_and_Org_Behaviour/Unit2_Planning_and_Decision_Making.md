## 1. Nature & Purpose of Planning

> **Definition:** Planning is the foundational management function of deciding in advance **what to do, how to do it, when to do it, and who is to do it**. It bridges the gap between where the organization is and where it wants to be.

### Key Characteristics of Planning:
1. **Primary Function:** Precedes organizing, staffing, directing, and controlling.
2. **Goal-Oriented:** Focused entirely on achieving specific corporate objectives.
3. **Pervasive:** Required across all levels and departments of an organization.
4. **Continuous & Forward-Looking:** Dynamically adapts to shifting external market conditions.

## 3. Types of Plans: Strategic vs. Tactical vs. Operational

* **Strategic Plans:** Long-term (3-5+ years), enterprise-wide plans drafted by Top Management.
* **Tactical Plans:** Medium-term (1-3 years), departmental plans designed by Middle Management.
* **Operational Plans:** Short-term (day-to-day / weekly / monthly), granular task plans for First-Line managers.

---

## 4. Management by Objectives (MBO) (Peter Drucker, 1954)

* A collaborative management approach where managers and employees jointly define common goals, define each individual's major areas of responsibility, and use these measures as guides for assessing performance.
* **SMART Goals Framework:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

---

## 5. The Decision-Making Process & Decision Conditions

### 6 Steps in Rational Decision-Making:
1. Define the problem.
2. Identify decision criteria.
3. Allocate weights to criteria.
4. Develop alternative options.
5. Evaluate alternatives.
6. Select, implement, and evaluate the decision outcome.

### Conditions for Decision-Making:
* **Certainty:** Outcomes of each alternative are fully known and predictable.
* **Risk:** Outcomes are not certain, but objective mathematical probabilities can be assigned (e.g., probability of rain = 40%).
* **Uncertainty:** Future outcomes are unpredictable, and probabilities cannot be assigned.
