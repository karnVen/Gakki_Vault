## 1. Definition & Nature of Management

> **Definition:** Management is the art and science of planning, organizing, directing, staffing, and controlling human, financial, physical, and informational resources to achieve organizational goals effectively and efficiently.

### Efficiency vs. Effectiveness (High-Yield 5-Mark Question):
* **Efficiency:** Doing things right — minimizing resource waste, cost, and time (Input-to-Output ratio).
* **Effectiveness:** Doing the right things — attaining organizational goals and delivering customer value.

## 2. Levels of Management & Required Skills

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐   Conceptual Skills (Strategic Vision)
│    Top Management (CEO, Board)  │         ▲
├─────────────────────────────────┤         │
│   Middle Management (HOD, Mgr)  │   Human / Interpersonal Skills (Team Leadership)
├─────────────────────────────────┤         │
│ First-Line / Operative (Super)  │         ▼
└─────────────────────────────────┘   Technical Skills (Hands-on Tools/Coding)
```

1. **Top-Level Management:** Establishes strategic vision, long-range goals, and company-wide policies. Requires highest **Conceptual Skills**.
2. **Middle-Level Management:** Translates top strategies into departmental tactical plans; coordinates teams. Requires high **Human / Relational Skills**.
3. **First-Line / Operative Management:** Directly oversees daily operational staff. Requires highest **Technical Skills**.

---

## 3. The 5 Core Functions of Management: POSDC Framework

1. **P - Planning:** Formulating goals, strategies, and actions in advance.
2. **O - Organizing:** Structuring tasks, assigning authority, and allocating departmental resources.
3. **S - Staffing:** Recruiting, selecting, training, evaluating, and compensating employees.
4. **D - Directing (Leading):** Motivating, communicating, and guiding team members toward objectives.
5. **C - Controlling:** Measuring actual performance against baselines and applying corrective actions.

## 4. Evolution of Management Thought (Guaranteed 10-Mark Question)

### 1. F.W. Taylor's Scientific Management (Early 1900s):
* Replaced "rule-of-thumb" guesswork with scientific study of work tasks.
* Four Principles: Science of work, scientific selection of workers, division of work, and mental revolution.

### 2. Henri Fayol's 14 Administrative Principles of Management:
1. Division of Work
2. Authority & Responsibility
3. Discipline
4. Unity of Command (Each employee receives orders from only ONE boss)
5. Unity of Direction (One plan, one head)
6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest
7. Remuneration
8. Centralization
9. Scalar Chain (Hierarchy of authority line)
10. Order
11. Equity
12. Stability of Tenure of Personnel
13. Initiative
14. Esprit de Corps (Team spirit and harmony)

### 3. Elton Mayo's Hawthorne Studies:
* Proved that social factors, informal groups, and human relationships impact worker productivity far more than physical lighting or financial incentives alone.
